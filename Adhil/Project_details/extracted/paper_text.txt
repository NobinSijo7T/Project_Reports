Abstract—Security and surveillance networks face critical challenges in various terms.There are lack of efficient end to end anomaly detection and multi camera tracking mod- ules.Traditional deep learning architecture often in this regard is not optimized in terms of power usage for anomaly detection frameworks and also tracking of people across various systems accordingly is not an integrated but a standalone system. This pa- per presents a hybrid framework that integrates a CNN-Spiking Neural Network (SNN) for anomaly detection with an event- triggered multi-camera person tracking module integrated. The anomaly detection module initially processes 16-frame clips using a MobilenetV3 backed backbone followed by SNN Parametric Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (PLIF) neurons trained with surrogate gradients.When the anomaly is detected , the system activates a tracking pipeline comprising YOLOv8, ByteTrack, and OSNet for person re-identification across multiple cameras. Experiments on the UCF-Crime dataset resulted that the detection module achieves 90.1% accuracy, 91.61% recall, and an AUC of 0.95 while consuming only 13.34 W on an NVIDIA T4 GPU— which is large energy reduction compared to conventional 3D CNNs making it suitable for large-scale, privacy-preserving surveillance deployments. The Re-ID subsystem (OSNet) yields a Rank- 1 accuracy of 94.02%, and mAP of 86.62% on the rearranged test set that is structured in such a way that is to reflect the expected ByteTrack tracklet output , where as on the same dataset without tracklet output structurisation the accuracy was below 40 which shows the importance of this framework method.

I. INTRODUCTION
A. Background
In the today’s world there is a rapid progress in use of cctv cameras from houses to institutions for monitoring and assessing security.When smart cities produce large petabytes of data the architecture and further advancements which enhances security or atleast give a boost to it; lags behind in the race. Most of the current surveillance systems rely on centralized cloud-based analysis, where high-resolution video streams have to be transmitted to remote servers. This approach introduces severe bottlenecks: such as excessive network bandwidth consumption,latency,and privacy risks due to persistent transmission of identifiable visual data.
These architectural challenges are further worsened by the computational inefficiency of conventional deep learning mod- els, which is never nearly compatible with edge deployment
and often dissipating large amount of power,which is some- thing that cannot be compromised on a system intended to work 24*7. The architectures seen currently result in increased energy consumption and standalone systems which is not providing an end to end services.
B. Motivation
This research is motivated by several practical limitations observed in current large-scale surveillance systems. First, monitoring CCTV footage becomes increasingly arduous and inefficient as the number of cameras grows, placing a signif- icant burden on human operators. Second, there is a growing need for power-efficient and scalable models inspired by biological vision systems. Third, most existing surveillance architectures operate as standalone systems, lacking a unified end-to-end framework that integrates anomaly detection and tracking across multiple camera views.
To address these challenges, we propose an end-to-end inte- grated anomaly detection framework based on a hybrid CNN– SNN architecture, complemented by re-identification (Re-ID) based tracking. In particular, Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) offer a promising direction due to their event-driven and sparse computation paradigm, which significantly improves energy efficiency compared to conventional artificial neural networks (ANNs). This property makes SNNs well-suited for surveillance scenarios, where visual scenes often remain static for extended periods and only sparse temporal changes are of interest.
Furthermore, recent advances in efficient convolutional ar- chitectures, such as MobileNetV3, enable lightweight spatial feature extraction, making the overall system more suitable for real-world deployment. By combining such efficient CNN backbones with temporally adaptive spiking neurons that model both fast and slow dynamics, the proposed system effectively captures temporal anomalies while maintaining computational efficiency.
In addition, advances in person re-identification (Re-ID) have enabled robust cross-camera identity matching, thereby overcoming tracking discontinuities between non-overlapping camera views. Integrating these components into a unified framework allows for scalable, energy-efficient, and intelligent surveillance systems capable of both anomaly detection and consistent multi-camera tracking.

C. Contributions
We propose a unified CNN–SNN based anomaly detection model integrated with Re-ID for cross-camera tracking:
• System A: A hybrid CNN–SNN anomaly detector that continuously monitors video streams by processing 16- frame clips. The model employs a lightweight Mo- bileNetV3 backbone for efficient spatial feature extrac- tion, followed by a feature reduction and spatial attention module. Temporal dynamics are modeled using a dual- timescale spiking neural network composed of paramet- ric Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (PLIF) neurons, capturing both fast and slow temporal variations. This is further enhanced with temporal attention and adaptive pooling for robust video-level anomaly prediction.
• System B: A multi-camera tracker that activates upon confirmed anomaly detection, performing real-time per- son detection (YOLOv8n, two-tier confidence pool- ing), two-stage temporal association (ByteTrack), crop quality filtering with CLAHE preprocessing, cross- camera re-identification (OSNet-AIN [17]), Mahalanobis- filtered tracklet centralization, and metadata-driven spatio-temporal trajectory reconstruction. The key contributions are:
• A novel energy-efficient hybrid MobileNetV3 + spiking neural network architecture that combines lightweight convolutional feature extraction with dual-timescale tem- poral spiking dynamics and attention mechanisms for improved anomaly detection.
• Integration of spatial attention, temporal attention, and learnable temporal weighting to enhance both frame-level and video-level anomaly prediction.
• A training strategy incorporating weighted focal loss to effectively handle class imbalance in real-world anomaly datasets such as UCF-Crime.
• Comprehensive evaluation on the UCF-Crime dataset, including ablation studies and comparisons with state- of-the-art approaches.

II. RELATED WORK
A. Video Anomaly Detection
Many earlier versions of video anomaly detection models relied mostly on handcrafted features and rule-based sys- tems.Many methoods such as Background subtraction methods like Gaussian Mixture Models (GMM) and kernel density
estimation formed the basis of motion-based anomaly detec- tion.Even though it was effective in controlled environments, these methods still struggled with dynamic backgrounds, illu- mination variations, and complex scene semantics.
Optical flow-based ideas later emerged to capture motion patterns by estimating pixel-wise velocity fields. Even though it was capable of identifying abnormal motion, these methods were still computationally expensive and highly sensitive to noise. Sparse reconstruction techniques afterwards made anomaly detection as an outlier detection problem; however, they required extensive modeling of normal behavior and showcased very poor generalization across varied scenes.
The sudden spurt of deep learning however significantly transformed the field of video surveillance systems. Convolu- tional Neural Networks widely known as CNNs enabled robust feature learning for object detection and activity recognition. Two-stage detectors such as Faster R-CNN and single-stage detectors like YOLO provided high accuracy but demanded huge computational resources often least concerned on the power efficiency.
Reconstruction-based deep learning methods, which in- cludes autoencoders [1], Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), and Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), became pop- ular for anomaly detection as later. Despite improved per- formance, these approaches process every frame exhaustively demanding higher spectra of computational efficiency, ir- respective of anomaly presence, leading to inefficiency in computational resource utilization.
Sultani et al. [2] put forward weakly-supervised multi- ple instance learning on the large-scale UCF-Crime dataset, achieving significant improvements. Three-dimensional CNNs and temporal networks addressed spatiotemporal modeling but introduced computational costs exceeding 100 GFLOPs per clip, which is devoid of efforts in the direction of power efficiency.
B. Multi-Camera Tracking Systems
Person Re-Identification i.e Re-ID evolved from basic hand- crafted descriptors to the more complicated deep metric learn- ing approaches. Earliest deep Re-ID models formulated the task as a classification problem, while later methods that followed focused more on learning discriminative embeddings. Hermans et al. [3] put forth the capabilities of triplet loss for learning discriminative embeddings. Zheng et al. [12] gave comprehensive surveys of Re-ID techniques.
Usual Multi-Target and Multi-Camera Tracking (MTMCT) mechanisms integrate actions such as detection, tracking, and Re-ID. YOLO variants [4] produce detection of subject to be tracked, while ByteTrack [5] high performance temporal association through motion prediction and IoU matching. OSNet [6] and its domain-adaptive variant OSNet-AIN [17] achieve state-of-the-art Re-ID performance through omni-scale feature learning, with OSNet-AIN additionally incorporating Aggregated Instance Normalization for improved robustness to cross-camera domain shift. However, all these systems assume continuous operation, which heeds unnecessary data resulting

C. Neuromorphic Computing in Vision
Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) represent a biologically motivated brain-inspired computing mechanisms where infor- mation is transmitted via discrete spikes rather than contin- uous ones which is prevalent in traditional ANNs(Artificial Neural Network).SNNs being activated only when have to be spiked,i.e when membrane potentials exceed defined thresh- olds hence reducing neural spikes to the values of 0s and 1s ,i.e discrete values . Primarily SNN research focused on simple datasets such as MNIST. Recent advancements in surrogate gradient learning [7] have enabled the training of deeper SNNs which would have otherwise relied on traditional ANN-SNN conversion methods involving larger time-steps and decreased accuracy . Kundu et al. [8] applied SNNs to temporal action recognition, but most applications remain limited to basic classification tasks,object detection tasks or relied on specialized event-based cameras.
D. Research Gaps
The literature reveals several research gaps:
• Computational Efficiency vs Accuracy: High-accuracy models require large computational resources.Existing systems fail to balance both aspects i.e accuracy and power efficiency.
• Continuous vs Event-Driven Processing: Most tracking systems operate continuously, even though anomalies constitute very less in a normal CCTV footage. No prior work integrates anomaly detection as a gating mechanism for tracking activation.
• Limited SNN Applications in Surveillance: Possibilities of SNN models has not been fully exploited in diverse fields and prevailing models do not address complex human behavior analysis in real-world surveillance. This work directly addresses these gaps through an inte- grated SNN based anomaly detection and tracking framework.
III. PROPOSED METHODOLOGY
A. System Overview
The framework proposed in this work consists of two com- plementary system modules designed for seamless integration. System A unceasingly monitors video streams using a hybrid CNN-SNN anomaly detector. When an anomaly is detected by the module with confidence exceeding thresholdψθa, System A triggers System B,Upon anomaly confirmation, System A extracts and saves the cropped person image(s) from the flagged frames, together with their bounding-box coordinates and frame-level metadata, into a designated query folder on disk, which System B subsequently consumes as its structured input to initiate the tracking and re-identification pipeline.. This design ensures that computationally expensive tracking operations are executed only for frames with anomalies de- tected.
B. System A: Hybrid CNN-SNN Anomaly Detector

Association proceeds in two stages. In Stage 1, all active and lost tracks are predicted forward, and a cost matrix is computed against the high-confidence detection pool using a score-fused IoU cost:

The Hungarian algorithm solves the assignment with threshold 1←−τm, whereψτm = 0.8 is the match threshold. In Stage 2, tracks unmatched in Stage 1 are re-matched against the low- confidence pool using pure IoU cost, recovering occluded or
weakly detected persons. Unmatched high-confidence detec- tions surviving both stages initialize new tracks with mono- tonically increasing identifiers. Lost tracks are retained for up to 30 frames before permanent deletion.
4) Crop Extraction and Quality Filtering: For each con- firmed track per frame, the bounding box is clamped to frame boundaries and the person crop is saved alongside per-frame metadata (frame index, timestamp, bounding box coordinates, and relative image path).
Prior to feature extraction, each crop undergoes quality validation. A frame is accepted only if it satisfies all of the following criteria: minimum crop size←≥40←× 40 px, aspect ratio within [0.2, 2.0], Laplacian variance←≥30 (blur threshold), exactly one person detected at confidence←≥0.5. Frames failing one or more criteria enter a salvage procedure: blurry frames are sharpened; frames with invalid aspect ratios are padded to meet the proportion constraint; frames with low detection confidence undergo CLAHE-based contrast enhance- ment and are re-validated. Frames that remain non-conforming after salvage are discarded.

IV. EXPERIMENTAL SETUP
A. Dataset
We use the UCF-Crime dataset [2], containing 500 untrimmed surveillance videos spanning 14 classes (13 anomaly types + normal). Table I summarizes the dataset statistics.

Property Value
Total Videos 500 Classes 14 (13 anomaly + normal) Total Duration ≈42 hours Average Video Duration 302.28 seconds Frame Rate 30 FPS Resolution 320×240 (65%), others Anomaly Types Assault, Burglary, Fighting, etc.
The dataset exhibits significant class imbalance, reflecting real-world surveillance conditions where normal activities vastly outnumber anomalous events.
B. Preprocessing Pipeline
1) Frame Extraction: Uniform sampling to extract 16- frame clips. For videos shorter than 16 frames, we pad with the last frame. 2) Resizing: All frames resized to 224×224 using bilinear interpolation with aspect-ratio padding. 3) Normalization: Using ImageNet statistics: mean = [0.485, 0.456, 0.406], std = [0.229, 0.224, 0.225]. 4) Data Augmentation:
• Random horizontal flip (p=0.5)
• Random rotation (±10◦)
• Color jitter (brightness=0.2, contrast=0.2, satura- tion=0.2)
• Random temporal reversal (p=0.5)
• Random temporal cropping
C. Train-Validation-Test Split
We employ stratified 5-fold cross-validation to address class imbalance:
• Training: 70% (45,000 clips)
• Validation: 15% (15,000 clips)
• Testing: 15% (15,000 clips) The split preserves class distribution across all folds.

H. Discussion
1) Strengths:
• Energy Efficiency: 87% reduction compared to CNN- based models enables long-term edge deployment.
Fig. 6: Qualitative Examples of Anomaly Detection
• Event-Driven Processing: Computational resources ac- tivated only upon confirmed anomalies, saving 94% of tracking computation.
• Privacy Preservation: Local processing with feature- only data transmission ensures personally identifiable information never leaves the edge device.
• Scalability: Supports deployment across large-scale dis- tributed camera networks with minimal infrastructure costs.
• Real-Time Operation: Sustains 28 FPS on edge hard- ware, meeting real-time surveillance requirements.
2) Limitations:
• Dataset Bias: Limited viewpoint and environmental di- versity in UCF-Crime may affect generalization.
• SNN Training Complexity: Surrogate gradients intro- duce approximation errors, requiring careful hyperparam- eter tuning.
• Absence of Person-Level Localized Anomaly Annota- tions: The UCF-Crime dataset provides only video- or frame-level anomaly labels, with no person-level bound- ing box annotations identifying which individual is re- sponsible for the detected anomaly. Consequently, the system can determine that an anomalous event occurs within a scene but cannot directly attribute it to a specific tracked person during training. This limits supervised optimization of the end-to-end pipeline at the person level, and the re-identification and tracking components are evaluated independently rather than in a jointly su- pervised anomaly-to-person localization framework.

VI. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK
A. Summary
We propose a neuromorphic edge intelligence framework that combines a hybrid CNN-SNN anomaly detection module with event-driven multi-camera tracking. Our system achieves an accuracy of 88.97% and a recall of 91.61% on the UCF-Crime dataset with a power consumption of 3.1 W, reflecting an 87% reduction in energy compared to traditional 3D CNN methods. The tracking pipeline employs two-stage ByteTrack association with OSNet-AIN-based re-identification [17], achieving a Rank-1 accuracy of 94.02% and mAP of 86.62% on the rearranged ReID test set. Mahalanobis-filtered tracklet centralization further improves cross-camera identity matching robustness. The event-based system achieves a 94% reduction in tracking computations and a 96.5% reduction in bandwidth, allowing for real-time execution on edge devices at 28 frames per second.
B. Key Outcomes
• Validation of SNN efficiency for complex surveillance tasks.
• Demonstration of event-driven tracking with 94% com- putation savings.
• Real-time performance at 28 FPS on edge devices.
• Scalable distributed architecture for camera networks.
• Privacy-preserving local analytics with metadata-only transmission.
• Robust cross-camera re-identification via OSNet-AIN with Mahalanobis-filtered tracklet centralization, achiev- ing Rank-1 accuracy of 94.02% and mAP of 86.62%.
C. Future Research Directions
• Dataset Expansion: Extend to larger, multi-country surveillance datasets (e.g., ShanghaiTech, CUHK Av- enue) for improved generalization.
• Transformer-SNN Hybrids: Integrate temporal trans- formers with SNN layers for better long-range depen- dency modeling.
• Hardware Acceleration: Deploy on FPGA and ASIC neuromorphic platforms for further energy savings.
• Multimodal Fusion: Incorporate audio and thermal sen- sors for robust detection under adverse conditions.
