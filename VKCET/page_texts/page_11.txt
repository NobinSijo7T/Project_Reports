The need for autonomous workflows becomes stronger when the environment is dynamic.
New tasks may arrive, priorities may change, or an agent may become unavailable. A fixed
workflow may not respond quickly to these changes. Dynamic task allocation allows work to
be moved to another suitable agent. Communication allows agents to share status and
information. Cooperation allows them to work toward a common objective.
The reference paper therefore focuses on a combination of multi-agent systems and Agentic AI
frameworks rather than treating agents as isolated programs.
1.3 Basic Terminology
An AI Agent is an AI-based entity that performs a specific task. A Multi-Agent System is a
collection of multiple agents that work in the same problem environment. Agentic AI refers to
an approach in which AI systems can perform tasks with greater autonomy, make decisions
and coordinate actions.
A workflow is an ordered set of tasks required to achieve an overall goal. Task decomposition
means breaking a large task into smaller subtasks. Task allocation means assigning those
subtasks to suitable agents. Dynamic task allocation means changing those assignments when
the situation changes. Communication is the exchange of information between agents, while
cooperation means working together to achieve a shared objective.
1.4 Scope of the Seminar
The seminar concentrates on autonomous workflows formed by multiple cooperating AI
agents. It focuses on agent roles, task decomposition, task assignment, autonomous execution,
communication, cooperation, dynamic reassignment and performance evaluation.
The seminar does not claim to implement a new production - ready MAWS. The report
explains and analyzes the design presented in the supplied 2026 IEEE reference paper and
organizes its concepts into a clear workflow-oriented model. Reported numerical results are
presented as reference-paper results rather than as experiments conducted for this seminar.
