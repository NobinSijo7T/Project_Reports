INTRODUCTION
1.1 General Background
Artificial Intelligence has developed from systems that mainly execute predefined instructions
toward systems that can reason about tasks and act with greater autonomy. An AI agent can be
understood as an AI system designed to perform a particular task. When several agents with
different roles operate together and exchange information, the result is a Multi-Agent System
(MAS).
Complex real-world workflows often contain many related activities. Some tasks may be
independent and can be performed in parallel, while others depend on information produced by
earlier tasks. Traditional workflow systems frequently rely on fixed sequences, manual task
assignment, or centralized control. Such arrangements can become difficult to manage when
the work load changes, resources become unavailable, or new tasks arrive.
Multi-Agent Autonomous Workflow Systems address this problem by treating the workflow
as a team activity. Instead of requiring one system or one person to manage every step, the
overall task can be divided into smaller subtasks. Specialized agents can then perform different
subtasks, communicate useful information, and adjust task assignments when conditions
change. The supplied reference paper presents this approach as a way to improve flexibility,
scalability and decision-making in changing environments.
1.2 Need for Multi-Agent Autonomous Workflows
A complex work flow may require different types of expert is eat the same time. For example,
a large business workflow may include information gathering, analysis, planning, resource
allocation and reporting. Giving every task to a single agent can make the system less flexible
and may create a bottleneck. A multi-agent approach distributes responsibilities across agents.
