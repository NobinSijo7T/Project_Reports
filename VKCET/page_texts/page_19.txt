3.8 Learning Mechanisms
The reference paper identifies supervised learning, unsupervised learning and reinforcement
learning as useful learning mechanisms. Reinforcement learning is particularly important for
autonomous decision-making because agents can learn from feedback received after actions.
Deep learning can support complex data processing, while collaborative multi-agent learning
can allow agents to learn or improve through interaction with other agents.
3.9 Adaptability
Adaptability means the ability to change actions when the environment changes. In MAWS,
changing workload, new tasks, resource availability or changing task requirements can trigger a
different allocation.
The reference paper gives examples such as rerouting delivery activities during unexpected
traffic conditions and real locating healthcare  resources  when patient demand changes.
3.10 Cooperation
Agents can work independently but still cooperate toward a common goal. Cooperation can
involve sharing information, sharing resources, coordinating plans and helping complete tasks
that are difficult for a single agent.
A successful multi-agent workflow therefore requires both local decision-making and system-
level coordination.
3.11 Degree of Autonomy
The level of agent independence can be adjusted according to the task. Some tasks may permit
agents to make decisions without continuous human involvement. Other tasks may require
greater supervision or approval.
