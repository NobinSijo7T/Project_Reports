Step 5 – Communicate
During execution, agents exchange useful information such as progress, task results, status and
resource changes. Communication allows one agent's output to become useful input for
another agent.
Step 6 – Reassign When Needed
If the environment changes, the original allocation may no longer be suitable. The system can
reassign tasks based on the new state. Examples include a change in priority, increased
workload or an unavailable agent.
Step 7 – Produce the Final Result
After subtasks are completed, the outputs are combined or coordinated to produce the overall
workflow result. The final result represents the collective work of the agents rather than the
output of only one agent.
4.2 Working Example
Consider a generic logistics workflow. The overall objective is to complete a set of deliveries.
One group of agents can handle delivery information, another can handle route-related
decisions, and another can monitor resource or vehicle status. If a route becomes unsuitable,
the system can reassign work or update the plan. The example illustrates the principle of
specialization, communication and dynamic adaptation without depending on a specific
software implementation.
