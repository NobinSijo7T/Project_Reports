2.8 Hierarchical Task Networks
Hierarchical Task Networks (HTNs) are discussed as a method for decomposing complex
tasks into simpler subtasks. This is closely related to MAWS because a large workflow can
first be represented at a high level and then divided into manageable units that can be assigned
to different agents.
The approach is especially relevant to workflows in which several dependent activities must be
coordinated.
2.9 Coordination and Mechanism Design
The reference paper discusses game theory, auction theory and mechanism design as
approaches that have been used to improve coordination and resolve conflicts among agents.
These approaches can help agents make decisions about resources and task allocation when
multiple agents compete for or depend on the same resources.
2.10 Research Gap and Motivation
The literature and reference paper indicate several practical gaps. Many workflows still depend
on manual control and fixed task assignment. Traditional centralized systems can be less
flexible when the workload or environment changes. As the number of tasks and agents grows,
coordination and communication become more difficult.
